Key principles, notifying DVLA and GMC guidance

Licence groups

  • Group 1: cars and motorcycles.
  • Group 2: large lorries (C) and buses (D). Car licences issued before 1 January 1997 also carry C1 (medium lorry) and D1 (minibus) entitlement; Group 2 standards apply to these on renewal.
  • Police, ambulance, fire and taxi drivers hold the same licences; employers and councils may set higher standards.

Risk thresholds

  • A condition likely to cause a sudden disabling event at the wheel means the person must not drive.
  • DVLA's thresholds: 20% risk of an event in a year for Group 1, 2% for Group 2.

The 3-month rule

  • Drivers generally need not notify DVLA of conditions where clinical advice is less than 3 months off driving, unless a specific standard says otherwise.

How the patient notifies

  • England, Scotland and Wales: gov.uk/driving-medical-conditions.
  • Northern Ireland (DVA): nidirect.gov.uk.
  • Driving against medical advice may invalidate their insurance. Record the advice clearly.
  • A licence holder may keep their entitlement to drive under Section 88 while DVLA makes enquiries (not if their last licence was revoked or refused on medical grounds). The treating doctor should advise whether it is safe to drive in the meantime.

Advice for clinicians

  • DVLA's doctors: medadviser@dvla.gov.uk (healthcare professionals only).
  • Medical notification form for when a patient cannot or will not notify.

GMC: when a patient keeps driving

  1. Explain the condition's effect on driving and their legal duty to tell DVLA.
  2. Tell them you may have to disclose to DVLA, in confidence, if they keep driving when unfit.
  3. Record the advice in their notes.
  4. If they cannot understand the advice (for example dementia), inform DVLA as soon as practicable.
  5. If they refuse to accept it, offer a second opinion and advise them not to drive meanwhile.
  6. If they continue to drive and this exposes others to a risk of death or serious harm, contact DVLA's medical adviser promptly. Try to tell the patient first, and confirm in writing afterwards.
